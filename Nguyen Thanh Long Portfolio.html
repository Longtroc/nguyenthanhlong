Project 01 · Supply Chain Simulation
The Fresh Connection
End-to-end supply chain simulation game which required managing ROI, service levels, inventory decisions, and supplier strategy across 6 competitive rounds. Qualified for the Global Student Challenge 2026.

What went wrong
Sales overpromised 95% service level: far beyond what operations could actually deliver, causing penalties and instability.
High safety stock + large batch sizes → obsolescence climbing to 5–8% and warehouse overflow.
Unreliable suppliers (PET, Vitamin C) added variability that forced even higher buffers everywhere.
Plan adherence around 78%: breakdowns and overtime kept pushing production into reactive mode.
Each department optimizing for itself → total supply chain cost went up and ROI hit −7.7%.
How we fixed it
Aligned service levels to actual capability: fewer penalties, more realistic commitments to customers.
Cut safety stock selectively + shorter production runs → obsolescence down, warehouse freed up.
Better capacity planning: targeted reduction in breakdowns and changeover time.
Dual sourcing for unreliable suppliers to balance cost against risk.
Lean for stable SKUs, responsive for risky ones: right strategy per product, not one-size-fits-all.
ROI moved from −7.7% to +8.2%: mainly through cost reduction and cross-functional alignment

Identify Value
Focus on what customers actually pay for. Picking and transport are value-added. Waiting and handoff delays are not: they just inflate lead time.
🗺️
Map the Waste
Stage delay data shows DIRECT orders have the highest pick time AND delay. Inbound trailer clustering at 9–11h creates a congestion wave that ripples through the whole shift.
🧩
Root Cause
It's not a capacity problem: the volume alone doesn't explain the delay pattern. The issue is how and when work arrives, not how much of it there is.
What the data showed
38.41% late shipment rate: flat across the entire Feb–Mar period. Not improving without intervention.
DIRECT order type has the highest avg pick time and stage delay: it's the main bottleneck across all carriers.
Trailer peak at 9–11h: when too many arrive at once, dock processing backs up and the queue never fully clears.
Inbound SLA at 73.96%: that 26% gap points to systemic dock inefficiency, not just occasional volume spikes.
Recommendations
Spread trailer arrival windows away from the 9–11h peak: even 30-min staggering would flatten the dock load significantly.
Dedicated DIRECT order lane: separate picking route and staging area for the highest-volume, highest-delay order type.
Pre-stage orders before the shift starts: eliminates the 0.5–0.7h stage delay that's currently baked into every order.
Visual Kanban signals between dock, picking, and dispatch: stops queue build-up before it becomes a problem.
